workflow

A personal application operated by Unsubject LLC · Last updated October 5, 2026

workflow connects Simon Lee’s own Google account to his private journal and planning assistant. It is built for one person’s personal use. It is not offered to the public, and nobody else can sign up or connect an account.

Personal use only. Only one Google account, the owner’s, can be connected. The sign-in page is protected by a secret that only the owner holds, and there is no registration, sharing or public access.

What workflow does

Simon records thoughts, ideas and tasks by sending messages to a private Telegram bot. workflow brings in context from his Google account so that this journal can:

  • Link journal entries to the people, events, tasks and emails they mention.
  • Turn notes into tasks. Simon can add tasks to Google Tasks from the chat. When a journal entry contains an action item, workflow suggests a task and adds it only if he confirms.
  • Keep an archive of his published writing. workflow gathers the columns and essays Simon has published, from the emails he sent to editors and from his own archive folders in Google Drive, so they can be cleaned up, searched and kept in one place.

Google data it uses, and why

  • Google Tasks (read and write): reads task lists to link journal entries to the tasks they mention, and creates the tasks added from the chat.
  • Google Calendar (read-only): reads events from 30 days back to 30 days ahead, with their titles, descriptions, times, locations and attendees, to link journal entries to events.
  • Google Contacts (read-only): reads names, email addresses, phone numbers and notes, to recognize the people a journal entry mentions.
  • Gmail (read-only): reads emails Simon sent in the last 30 days, starred emails, and family-related emails from the last 30 days. It uses the subject, sender, recipients, labels and plain-text body to link journal entries to the emails they relate to. It also reads every email, of any age, under the label Simon keeps for his writing, including attached Word documents, to rebuild the archive of his published columns and essays. It never sends, deletes or changes email.
  • Google Drive (read-only): reads the documents in the folders Simon designates (his article archive and his exports from WordPress and Substack) to rebuild the archive of his published writing. From a Substack export it reads only the posts, never the subscriber lists. It never creates, changes, shares or deletes files.

How the data is handled

  • Google data is stored in a private database that only the owner can reach.
  • To index and search the archive and answer Simon’s questions, relevant excerpts are sent to AI model providers (Anthropic and OpenAI) through their APIs. Their terms don’t let them train their models on this data.
  • The data is never sold, never used for advertising, and never used to train AI models.
  • workflow’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Details are in Section 3 of the Privacy Statement.

Disconnecting

Access can be removed at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. workflow then stops reading the account. Stored Google data is deleted on request.
